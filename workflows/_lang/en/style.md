# English style

How Jadwigo writes English: US English, drafted in English rather than translated from the
Polish. The reviewer checks lines against `checklist.md`; this file says how to write them so
they pass. A workflow writing English reads it before its first line.

## For a child of 6 to 9

- Short sentences, one idea each, in the active voice and the present tense. Speak to the child
  as `you`: `Count the groups again.`
- Every sentence ends with `.`, `?` or `!`, and a question with `?`.
- Kind and plain. Say what to try, never a bare `wrong` or `no`, and never that a task is easy
  (`easy`, `simple`, `just`).
- Praise what the child did, with its numbers: `You counted all 4 rows.` No `Awesome!` after a
  miss, no praise for being smart.
- Use the lesson's own signs and words, as the question uses them: `×` for times, `groups of`,
  `rows of`, `in all` (see `glossary.md`).
- A help card speaks to that miss on that question, with its numbers, and never prints the
  answer: `6 + 7 is adding. Here there are 7 groups of 6.`
- No names, no Polish.

## For a parent

- Short sentences, one idea each. Concrete: what the child did and what comes next, with code's
  own numbers.
- Kind, never alarming: `needs attention`, not `problem` or `weak`.
- Speak to the parent as `you`, and of the child as `your child`, `they`.
- No level names (`mastered`, `proficient`), no score, no percentage, no jargon.

## Numbers

Counts are digits (`18 questions`), with the noun plural from 2: `1 question`, `2 questions`.
Decimals take a point (`2.5`), thousands a comma (`1,200`).
