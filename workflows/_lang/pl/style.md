# Polish style

How Jadwigo writes Polish. The reviewer checks lines against `checklist.md`; this file says how
to write them so they pass. A workflow writing Polish reads it before its first line.

## For a child of 6 to 9

- Short sentences, one idea each. Address the child as `ty`: `Policz jeszcze raz grupy.`
- Every sentence ends with `.`, `?` or `!`, and a question with `?`.
- Kind and plain. Say what to try, never a bare `źle` or `błąd`, and never that a task is easy
  (`łatwe`, `proste`, `łatwizna`).
- A past-tense verb about the child takes the profile's form when the run names one, and is
  neutral otherwise: `Udało się.`, `Spróbuj jeszcze raz.`
- Praise what the child did, with its numbers: `Policzyłaś wszystkie 4 rzędy.` No `super!` after a
  miss, no praise for being clever.
- Use the lesson's own signs and words, as the question uses them: `·` for times, `razy`, `po`,
  `grupy`, `rzędy` (see `glossary.md`).
- A help card speaks to that miss on that question, with its numbers, and never prints the
  answer: `6 + 7 to dodawanie. Tu jest 7 grup po 6.`
- No names, no English.

## For a parent

- Short sentences, one idea each. Concrete: what the child did and what comes next, with code's
  own numbers.
- Kind, never alarming: `wymaga uwagi`, not `problem` or `słabo`.
- The form of address is not decided, so write impersonally: `Warto…`, `Następna lekcja…`. No
  `Pan`, `Pani` or `ty` forms.
- Write about the child as `dziecko`. No level names (`opanowane`, `biegle`), no score, no
  percentage, no jargon.

## Numbers

Counts are digits (`18 pytań`), with the noun in the case the numeral needs: `1 pytanie`,
`2 pytania`, `5 pytań`, `22 pytania`. Decimals take a comma: `2,5`.
