The learning loop is what Jadwigo does after the first lesson. A parent sets a goal, lessons
collect evidence, code turns the evidence into what the child knows, the next lesson is drawn from
that, and a short report tells the parent what moved. The parent says yes before anything changes.

  caption="One goal round its loop, with the parent deciding at the join."
/>

Today each step exists on its own and is walked by hand. 3 experiments ran the steps on 12 invented
learners. No real child's data was used.

## Knowing the learner

Every first try at a question becomes evidence. Code in `packages/core` folds that evidence into a
level for each skill, a flag when a skill needs attention, and a date when the skill is due again.
The due date comes from FSRS, a spaced-repetition scheduler. No model computes a level.

[E1](/experiments/e1/) folded 1,301 first tries over 21 days. The guesser, the child who is right
only with help, and the weekend-only child each came out clearly different. It also flagged 79 of
120 skill rows, so almost every plan says "reteach". That bias is the fold's next fix.

## Drawing the next lesson

A brief sorts a goal's skills into reteach, review, new and recheck. The draw then picks bank
questions the child hasn't seen, or ones the scheduler says are due, and records a reason for each
question it keeps or leaves out.

[E2](/experiments/e2/) drew a lesson for each learner from the real bank for $0.09. 11 of 12 got
15 to 18 questions, and 1 got nothing. A panel of model reviewers rated the fit at 2.39 of 5: lessons
ran long and retaught the wrong skills. The draw works, and the choice of what to draw doesn't yet.

## The parent's report

Code writes a digest: what moved, which answers moved it, the mistakes, and at most 3 proposals. A
model writes only the words the parent reads, in Polish or English. Code checks those words against
the digest before they're saved, and a proposal changes nothing until the parent accepts it.

[E3](/experiments/e3/) wrote 12 reports for $0.16. Model judges rated them 4.8 of 5 for accuracy
and 3.9 for warmth. Only 1 of 12 passed the code check, mostly on a field-name slip, and the judges
were the same model family as the writer, so their scores run high.

## Limits

- Nothing in the loop runs on its own yet. A person starts each step.
- The learners, their answers and the panel's ratings are all invented or model-made.
- A parent never sees a level name, a score or a percentage. The report says it in words.
