This experiment is for a judge asking what a learner should meet tomorrow: from each cohort
learner's state, the draw picks the next lesson from the real bank and gives a reason for every
question in or out.

<ExperimentResult id="e2" />

Each learner's brief from [E1](/experiments/e1/) becomes needs in brief order, and the lesson
takes the first 3 entries that draw questions. Bank sets are found by skill code in a local
checkout of the real bank (412 set pages scanned), so no hosted search ran and that step cost $0.
`jw draw` does the picking with its provisional rules (`rule_settled: false`): unseen questions
for practice, due-or-twin for review, spacing held. Only item ids, skills and reasons are
recorded here, never bank question text. A panel of 3 judges then rates each lesson for fit to
its learner (1 to 5, with reasons); every judge call ran through the Meta API on
`muse-spark-1.3-contributor` and is in the product ledger.

<Diagram name="next-lesson" caption="From a brief to a lesson, with a reason for every question." />

What the run seeds is the next-lesson button and the goals loop: a brief that becomes a lesson a
parent can check line by line before a child sees it.

## Every learner's next lesson

The panel column is the contributor panel's mean. The same draws were also rated by an earlier
`muse-wonder` panel, run before the owner's rule that scripted calls use the contributor models
on the API; its mean is kept here for a brief comparison, and in full in the JSON under
`detail.wonder_comparison`.

| Learner | Profile | Questions drawn | Fit, contributor panel | Fit, wonder panel |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Learner A | steady | 18 | 3 | 2.67 |
| Learner B | forgetful | 15 | 2.33 | 2.67 |
| Learner C | guesser | 18 | 3.33 | 2.33 |
| Learner D | slow-then-click | 18 | 2 | 3 |
| Learner E | help-heavy | 15 | 2 | 2 |
| Learner F | rusher | 18 | 3.33 | 2.67 |
| Learner G | perfectionist | 18 | 3 | 3.67 |
| Learner H | early-plateau | 15 | 1.33 | 2 |
| Learner I | weekend-only | 18 | 3.33 | 2 |
| Learner J | uneven | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| Learner K | comeback | 15 | 2 | 2 |
| Learner L | new-and-keen | 15 | 2 | 2 |

3 readings a judge can check in the JSON:

- 11 of 12 learners get a lesson of 15 to 18 questions. The uneven learner gets none: their
  brief is 4 Polish spelling skills, and no bank set carries those codes yet, plus a review
  entry with nothing due. The left-out reasons say exactly that.
- Both panels land in the same place: mean fit 2.39 (contributor) against 2.33 (wonder), and
  often for the same stated reason, that reteaching a skill already at proficient wastes the
  lesson while a weaker one is ignored, and that 18 questions is long for one sitting.
- The cheapest step was the draw itself. The 36 judge calls cost $0.0893 in the ledger; the
  bank lookup was local and free.
