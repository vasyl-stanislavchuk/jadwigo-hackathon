We use Jadwigo with our own son. I've been an engineer for more than 15 years, and at work I now run hundreds of AI agents. They still surprise me, so nothing they make reaches a child unchecked.

I'm also a dad of 2 crazy boys. We recently moved to Poland: new language, no grandparents, few friends. When homework goes badly, it's on us, in the evening, with no time. For David, it's multiplication and Polish spelling.

**What you get from that.**

- **A lesson for your child, not for the whole class.** Each child gets stuck somewhere different. Khan gives a child their own pace, but we needed their own content: what came home from school today. A photo of the homework becomes a lesson in a few minutes.
- **Questions you can trust.** Jadwigo takes questions from the question bank first, each with its source, and checks every answer. You see the lesson before your child does.
- **A calm place for your child.** No points, and no chatting with an AI. One link or a sheet of paper.

**Where the name comes from.** It's a play on words: Jadwiga, the queen, and "go", moving. Moving forward and growing under her warm wings. Queen Jadwiga is a school's patron. She was crowned "king" in 1384, and her jewels helped restore the Kraków Academy. In our pictures she's a little queen, and her pencil-case court stands beside her: Gumka the eraser, Ołówek the pencil and Linijka the ruler.

**What's next.** First David's class, then his school. And then maybe you, if you want to be close to your child's learning.
