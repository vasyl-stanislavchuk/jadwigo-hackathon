# Jadwigo: what it promises, to whom, and why we build it

Jadwigo's first users are one family: a mum, a dad and their son, who practises multiplication and Polish spelling at home. Everything in this drop starts there. On 27 October the owner put it this way: "The key audience for this project so far is me, my wife, and our kid."

## TL;DR

- **[Mission and value](promise.html):** Jadwigo puts the family at the centre. A parent sees the child grow, and each new lesson is built from what the child got right and wrong in the last one. The child learns at their own pace and can say "I want more" or "enough for today". The owner chooses from [3 mission options, 3 vision options and 4 lead lines](promise.html).
- **[Story](story.html):** a family builds Jadwigo for their son, then opens it to his class, then his school, and then to anyone who wants it. Jadwigo started at the Meta hackathon and is named after the patron of his school, Queen Jadwiga.
- **[How Jadwigo differs](compare.html):** Khan Academy personalises the pace, Duolingo builds on streaks, Kahoot is for groups and Quizlet runs on study sets. None of them is built around one child's goals with the parent beside them.
- **[Strategy](strategy.html):** do things that don't scale, let real goals decide what gets built, and reach families through people they trust, not ads.
- **[Voice](voice.html)** and **[Name story](name.html):** the copy, in 3 voices and 3 stories, all in Polish.
- **[What the owner decides](decide.html):** 5 choices, each filed as an issue, with options and no pick.

This drop has no illustrations, logo or palette. Both the look and a possible character wait for the design system, which merges later (`dl-w0b4.35`, `dl-w0b4.38`).
